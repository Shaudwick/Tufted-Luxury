Sun Card

In the Rider–Waite tradition, The Sun is the nineteenth card of the Major Arcana — and among the most luminous in the entire deck. It arrives after the trials of Death, the upheaval of the Tower, and the illusions of the Moon. What remains is clarity: unfiltered light, uncomplicated joy, and the quiet triumph of truth over fear.

Waite described a naked child riding a white horse beneath a radiant sun, holding a red banner — symbols of innocence regained, purity of intention, and the vital force of renewal. The sunflowers in the border turn toward the light as sunflowers do: growth that orients itself toward what nourishes. The stone wall behind them is not a prison but a boundary crossed — limitation left behind.

Sun Card tufts that archetype into wool and frame. The golden sun with its straight and wavy rays speaks of conscious clarity and living vitality working together. The rider moves forward without armor — visible, unafraid, alive in the warmth. The red banner is celebration made tangible: the courage to be seen in your joy.

Upright, The Sun signifies success, vitality, and authentic happiness — not perfection, but warmth worth trusting.

The light was never hidden.
Only waiting.
And now it rides forward.

The Magician

The Magician is the first numbered card of the Major Arcana — the moment after The Fool, when intention gathers form. In the Rider–Waite deck, a youthful figure stands with one hand raised toward heaven and the other pointing to earth. This is the Hermetic principle made visible: as above, so below. Spirit descends; matter answers. The Magician is the conduit between realms.

On the table before him lie the four symbols of the tarot suits — wand, cup, sword, and pentacle — representing fire, water, air, and earth. Waite wrote that these elements lie like counters before the adept, and he adapts them as he wills. Above his head, the lemniscate — the infinity symbol — marks unlimited potential and the eternal attainment of the spirit. Roses and lilies bloom at his feet: the culture of aspiration, ideas brought to fruition.

His white robe speaks of purity; his red mantle, of worldly experience earned. He does not reject the material world — he masters himself within it, and makes the invisible visible through deliberate will. Every loop in this tufted piece is a thread of that same principle: what you reach for in the spirit, you must also shape with your hands.

Upright, The Magician signifies manifestation, resourcefulness, and the power to begin — all tools present, all elements aligned.

Heaven is invoked.
Earth is addressed.
And the work takes form.
